## Part 2: Business, client, value, and pricing

### 1. Business Definition
* The business operates as an **influencer marketing brokerage agency** that acts as an intermediary (middleman) connecting content creators with target corporate brands seeking sponsorship and marketing partnerships.

---

### 2. Money Flow
* **Brand to Agency**: The brand pays the agency an agreed total campaign or integration fee (flat rate, CPM, or overall media budget).
* **Agency to Creator**: The agency pays the content creator a lower negotiated rate (flat fee or CPM) to produce and publish the sponsored integration.
* **Agency Margin / Commission**: The agency pockets the profit margin (the difference between what the brand paid and what the creator accepted) or charges a **10%–30% management/agency fee**.

---

### 3. Business Models & Niche Options
* **Deal Brokering / Single Creator (Foot-in-the-Door)**:
  * **Pros**: High profit margins on underpriced creators; low friction to close initial deals.
  * **Cons**: One-off transactional deals requiring upselling to build recurring revenue.
* **Roster Method / Campaign Sourcing**:
  * **Pros**: Pitching multiple creators increases total deal sizes and overall agency revenue.
  * **Cons**: Sending full rosters risks creator theft/backstabbing or overwhelming brand decision-makers.
* **Campaign / Budget Sourcing (Retainer Model)**:
  * **Pros**: High predictable monthly revenue (e.g., 10%–20% fee or managing \$50k–\$100k budgets).
  * **Cons**: Heavy liability and performance risk if campaign KPIs fail.
* **Talent Representation / Exclusivity**:
  * **Pros**: Secures inbound brand deals and 15%–40% steady cuts.
  * **Cons**: High operational headache; shifts focus away from deal brokering.

---

### 4. Why Choose an Agency Over Alternatives?
* **Vs. In-House Employee**: Avoids **\$50k+ annual salaries**, management overhead, and \$2k testing errors on unvetted creators.
* **Vs. Freelancers**: Saves massive internal time by handling manual creator search, audience auditing, legal contracts, script reviews, and rate negotiations.
* **Vs. Creator Marketplaces**: Marketplaces miss fast-growing micro/mid-tier creators, charge markups without managing logistics, and lack custom view guarantees or hands-on support.

---

### 5. Ideal Client Profile
* **Size & Stage**: Small to mid-sized growing brands or established companies scaling influencer channels (from 180 employees to lean startups).
* **Budget**: Ranging from **\$2k–\$5k pilot budgets** up to **\$10k, \$30k, \$50k, or \$100k monthly/quarterly spend**.
* **Industry**: Tech/SaaS (software), Gaming, Fitness/Health, DIY/Home Improvement, Automotive, and Productivity.
* **Decision-Maker Job Titles**: **Marketing Manager** (primary budget holder), Brand Manager, Head of Partnerships / Influencer Marketing Manager, Growth Marketer, Marketing Director, and CEO/Founder.

---

### 6. Biggest Problems, Frustrations, and Fears
* *"Pouring money into ads and seeing nothing come back"*.
* *"Getting scammed through a middleman transaction"*.
* Fear of *"ROI becoming a guessing game"*.
* Experiencing *"bad results"* where a *"campaign tanked"* or flopped due to *"unstable/fluctuating views"*.
* Dealing with *"bad agencies"* or *"unprofessional"* partners.
* Facing *"a big headache"* with slow, uncooperative creators or tedious negotiation back-and-forth.

---

### 7. Past Disappointments
* Running paid social ads (Meta/Google) that yielded flat engagement and poor return on ad spend.
* Working with *"bad"* or *"unprofessional"* agencies that charged high fees without delivering clear ROI.
* Direct outreach to creators or talent agencies that resulted in overpriced quotes, ghosting, or fake metrics.
* Sourcing from creator marketplaces/software that lacked manual vetting and hands-on campaign execution.

---

### 8. Desired Outcomes
* **Measurable Return on Investment (ROI)** and direct sales/revenue conversion.
* **Massive Brand Awareness**, reach, and visibility in front of their target demographic.
* High audience engagement and **guaranteed view counts**.
* A **risk-free, hands-off process** that saves internal operational time.

---

### 9. Objections & Recommended Answers
* **Price ("Too high")**: Isolate whether it is overall price or ROI uncertainty; offer a trial pilot or adjust campaign scope (e.g. 60s integration vs. dedicated video).
* **Trust / "No track record"**: Use creator social proof (*"this creator drove 3M views for Adobe"*), highlight logistics handling, or provide view guarantees.
* **"Will it work? / Risk of flopping"**: Provide a **View Guarantee** where additional integrations are delivered for free if view thresholds aren't met.
* **"We can do it ourselves"**: Use Aikido: explain *"We represent them for projects like this and eliminate your internal outreach and negotiation workload"*.

---

### 10. Purchase Triggers
* **End of Quarter / Unspent Budget**: Approaching the end of Q4 (or Q3/BFCM) where managers must deploy remaining budget or lock Q1 slots.
* **New Product or Feature Launch**: Launching new SaaS software, physical products, or seasonal promotions (Prime Day, Black Friday).
* **Ad Performance Fatigue**: Declining returns or rising costs on traditional paid ad channels.
* **Urgency / Limited Availability**: Holding a high-performing creator slot for a **5–7 day hold window**.

---

### 11. Concrete Results & Benefits
* **Views & Reach**: Millions of views (e.g. **3 million views with 16% engagement rate**).
* **ROI & ROAS**: **100% to 300% (1x to 3x+) Return on Investment**, generating \$20k–\$30k+ from a \$10k spend.
* **View Guarantees**: **50k, 100k, or 200k guaranteed views** within 30 to 45 days.
* **Execution Timelines**: Proposal to draft review in 7–15 days, and live campaign deployment within 14–30 days.

---

### 12. Work & Effort Avoided by Client
* Searching for and manually vetting creators for engagement and demographics.
* Cold outreach, follow-ups, and managing back-and-forth communication.
* Negotiating creator rates and pricing terms.
* Drafting, customizing, and enforcing legal contracts or non-compete clauses.
* Managing product shipping, script reviews, draft approvals, and live content tracking.

---

### 13. Realistic Execution Timeline
* **First Creator Introduction**: **1 to 3 days** using a foot-in-the-door pitch or handpicked 1–3 creator list.
* **Contract Signing & Product Delivery**: **1 to 5 days**.
* **Draft & Script Submission**: **7 to 15 days**.
* **Live Content Deployment**: **14 to 30 days (3–4 weeks)** total from contract signing to going live.

---

### 14. Risks Avoided & Removal Methods
* **Overpaying**: Agency benchmarks niche CPMs (e.g., \$25–\$50 CPM for tech) and negotiates creators below market rates.
* **Bad Fit & Fake Audiences**: Agency audits geographic metrics (e.g. 50%+ US/UK audience) and engagement before pitching.
* **No-Shows & Delays**: Enforces strict draft/posting deadlines in contracts and signs creators before brand contracts.
* **Underperforming Views / Flops**: Solved via **View Guarantees** (50k–100k views over 30–45 days) requiring creators to post free "make-good" content if missed.

---

### 15. Proof Points & Social Proof Angles
* **Creator Social Proof**: Leverage the creator's subscriber count, average views, and past brand collaborations (e.g., *"This creator produced 3M views for Adobe"*).
* **Foot-in-the-Door Pilot**: Pitch a single, highly aligned creator at an under-market test rate to eliminate decision barriers.
* **Professional Logistics**: Present clean briefs, clear deliverables, and structured timelines.
* **Emergency Social Proof Strategy**: Mention being part of an incubator/consulting network of multi-six-figure agency owners (without naming personal names).

---

### 16. Recommended Pricing Structures
* **Brokerage / Flat Fee (Recommended)**: Pitch brand a flat fee (\$3,000–\$10,000+), pay creator a lower flat rate (\$1,500–\$5,000), and keep the difference.
* **CPM Model**: Benchmark CPMs by platform (**YouTube \$20–\$50 CPM**; **IG Reels \$10–\$20 CPM**; **Facebook \$5–\$8 CPM**; **X/Twitter \$1–\$3 CPM**).
* **Hybrid Structure**: Low flat rate plus CPM performance bonus (e.g. \$1.5k flat + \$20 CPM).
* **Management Retainer / Campaign Spend**: Fixed monthly retainer or **10%–30% management fee** on campaign budget.

---

### 17. Guarantees & Risk-Reversals
* **View Guarantee**: Guarantee specific minimum views (e.g. 50k, 100k, or 80% of average views) over 30 to 45 days.
* **Free "Make-Good" Videos**: Creator posts additional integrations for free until the guaranteed view threshold is reached.
* **Pro-Rated View Guarantee**: Adjusting final payouts proportionally to delivered views.
* **Milestone Approval**: Payment released only upon draft review and verified upload.

---

### 18. Recommended Low-Friction First Step
* **Foot-in-the-Door Pilot Deal (1 tailored creator or 1–3 handpicked options)**.
* **Why?**:
  * Lowers financial and commitment risk for a new client.
  * Avoids confusing decision-makers with massive creator rosters.
  * Pre-handles objections before attempting larger budget asks.
  * Secures a quick win to prove ROI and upsell into multi-video campaigns or retainers.

---

### 19. Explaining Pricing to Clients
* **Flat All-Inclusive Price**: Quote a single price covering the deliverable without line-iteming creator costs or agency markups.
* **Do Not Disclose Internal Margins**: If asked, state that management and sourcing are included in the rate, or *"our fee is arranged separately"*.
* **Frame Around Value & ROI**: Explain rates based on audience alignment, engagement quality, and expected returns.
* **Transparent Management Fee**: When managing full client budgets, explicitly state a 10%–20% agency management fee on top of ad spend.

## Part 3: Process, creators, market, messaging, and trust

---

### 1. Client Journey from First Contact to Campaign End

* **Outreach & Discovery**: The agency reaches out or receives an inquiry, followed by a discovery call to gather the brand's criteria, goals, and target audience.
* **Shortlisting Creators**: The agency curates a shortlist of 1–3 pre-vetted creators with channel metrics and audience demographics; the brand reviews and approves the selections.
* **Contracts & Terms**: The brand sends its standard agreement to the agency; the agency reviews terms, adapts the rate/deliverables, and issues a separate creator agreement.
* **Protective Signing Order**: The creator signs the agency-creator contract first; once secured, the agency signs the brand-agency contract, and the brand provides the final brief and tracking links.
* **Content Production & Review**: The creator submits the script and draft; the agency verifies quality against the brief before passing it to the brand for final approval.
* **Going Live & Reporting**: The video posts on the scheduled date; the agency verifies promo links and provides performance updates (24h/48h–72h) and handles payment payouts.

---

### 2. Sourcing, Vetting, and Approaching Creators

* **Sourcing**: Creators are located organically via direct searches on YouTube and Instagram rather than restrictive database tools.
* **Vetting Criteria**:
  * **Views**: Minimum threshold of 30,000–50,000 average views per video.
  * **Engagement**: Healthy engagement rate of 3%–8% with active, organic comments.
  * **Demographics**: At least 35%–40% audience concentration in the primary target country (e.g., US).
  * **Consistency & Reliability**: Posts consistently (at least monthly) and responds promptly.
* **Approach Strategy**: Outreached via direct email or DM using plural "we" framing, referencing a specific recent video, presenting a clear offer, and ending with a simple question CTA.

---

### 3. "Warm Introductions" vs. Cold Outreach

* **Definition**: A warm introduction occurs when a mutual contact, friend, or relative connects the agency to a brand decision-maker, or when a brand lead has interacted with you previously.
* **Why It Is Better**: Cold outreach faces heavy guard walls, skepticism, and high friction because the brand does not know you. Warm introductions leverage pre-existing trust, lower the brand's guard, remove sales resistance, and significantly increase deal conversion rates.

---

### 4. Client Deliverables

* **Creator Shortlist**: A curated breakdown of 1–3 matched creators detailing audience demographics, average views, and engagement metrics.
* **Discovery & Strategy Call**: An intake call to establish campaign parameters, target demographics, and brand goals.
* **Contract & Campaign Brief**: Customized agreements locking in rates, go-live dates, usage rights, and creative guidelines.
* **Content Integration**: Final approved video deliverables (e.g., 60–90 second YouTube integrations, Reels, or dedicated videos).
* **Performance Report & Tracking**: Post-campaign updates tracking 24h/48h performance, view milestones, and promo/discount code redemptions.

---

### 5. Why Creators Work With Us & What They Care About Most

* **Why They Work With Us**: We bring them fully funded brand deals without them needing to perform cold outreach; we handle administrative work, contract logistics, and deal negotiations so they can focus strictly on content creation.
* **What They Care About Most**:
  * **Sponsorship Revenue**: Securing fair, paid brand partnerships.
  * **Fast & Reliable Payment**: On-time invoice payouts without delays.
  * **Brand Alignment**: Partnering with reputable products that fit their channel's style.
  * **Zero Friction**: Simple communication without tedious administrative back-and-forth.

### 6. Creator Rates & Fair Market Value in AI, SaaS, and Tech

* **YouTube CPM Benchmarks**: Standard tech niche CPM ranges from \$20 to \$50. AI niche CPM ranges from \$40 to \$70 for integrations, reaching \$100–\$200+ CPM because AI creators act as direct category leaders.
* **Short-Form Rates (Reels/TikTok)**: Priced at half to one-third of long-form YouTube CPMs (\$15–\$30 CPM standard, \$50–\$100 CPM in AI).
* **Rates by Size/Type**: In AI, creators averaging 40k–50k views frequently quote \$10,000–\$15,000 for dedicated videos or high-demand integrations.
* **Fair Market Rate Definition**: Calculated using the baseline CPM formula: `(Average Views / 1,000) * Niche CPM`. An offer matching standard niche CPM benchmarks is considered fair market value.

---

### 7. Brand-Creator Misunderstandings & How the Broker Fixes Them

* **Common Misunderstandings**:
  * **Pricing Friction**: Creators demand inflated flat fees based on self-perceived authority, while brands expect strict, lower CPMs.
  * **Communication Barriers**: Brands use corporate jargon and extensive requirement lists, whereas creators get frustrated by tedious media kit requests and long back-and-forth emails.
  * **Execution Gaps**: Tensions over creative freedom, script revisions, and delayed posting dates.
* **How the Broker Fixes Them**: Acts as the central buffer and decision-maker; negotiates creator rates down to fair CPM levels while framing value to the brand; handles contracts, script reviews, and deadlines seamlessly so neither side experiences friction.

### 12. Client Communication Tone, Words to Use, and Words to Avoid

* **Tone**: Professional, direct, concise, objective, and frictionless (written at a 4th-grade reading level).
* **Words to Use**: Use plural pronouns like "we" and "our" to sound established; reframe costs as "requirements" or "alignment"; use precise metrics like "audience analytics", "engagement rate", and "CPM".
* **Words to Avoid**:
  * Avoid "I", "my agency", or stating your agency name upfront.
  * Avoid defensive terms like "budget", "price", or "discount".
  * Avoid generic sales fluff ("circling back", "hope you're well", "top-tier audience").
  * Avoid agency tags in your company name/domain like "agency", "media", "digital", or "studios".

---

### 13. Agency Self-Presentation Mistakes

* **Generic Naming**: Using terms like "agency", "media", "digital", "studios", or personal names in company titles and email domains.
* **Self-Centric Messaging**: Writing lengthy emails about agency backstories instead of focusing 100% on the brand's value (violating the 1:3 self-to-recipient ratio).
* **Faking Scale**: Falsely claiming to be a large corporation, using grand signatures ("CEO", "Partnership Lead"), or making unrealistic sales promises.
* **Cluttered Communications**: Including website links, social icons, phone numbers, bold text, or heavy formatting in initial outreach that trigger spam filters.

### 15. Recommended Calls-to-Action (CTAs)

* **Recommended Format**: An ultra-short, single binary (yes/no) question ending strictly with a question mark.
* **Best-Converting Examples**:
  * "Open to partnerships?"
  * "Would you like me to send a short list of creators?"
  * "Should I send more details?"
  * "Are you open to more brand deals this quarter?"
* **CTAs to Avoid**: Multi-sentence calls-to-action, asking for a phone call on first touch, or adding text after the question mark.

### 16. Building Trust as a New Agency Without Clients

* **Transparent Pricing**: Use clear, fixed fee structures or standard 10% agency commissions.
* **Foot-in-the-Door Method**: Pitch a single high-fit creator for a small, low-risk pilot campaign rather than demanding \$20,000+ upfront.
* **Contract Protection**: Execute strict signing orders where the client signs first and terms are verified before handling funds.
* **Performance Guarantees**: Offer view milestone guarantees or make-good video commitments to remove downside risk.
* **Direct Alignment**: Demonstrate that specific creators are already vetted and eager to showcase the brand's exact product.
