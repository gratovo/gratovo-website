# Prompts used to extract the course knowledge

Each was pasted into a separate Gemini/NotebookLM chat. The answers are in `info about business.md` (Part 2 = prompt 1, Part 3 = prompt 2). Part 1 of that file holds the owner's own answers to the personal questions.

## Prompt 1: Business, client, value, and pricing

I'm rewriting the website copy for an influencer-marketing brokerage business based on this course. Answer from the course only, using its own wording, examples, and numbers. If something isn't covered, write "NOT COVERED" and don't guess. Use bullet points and keep each answer under 200 words.

1. In one plain sentence, what does this business do, and for whom?
2. Who pays whom, and for what? Explain every money flow (brand, creator, agency).
3. What different ways or models does the course say this business can be run or niched down? Give the pros and cons of each.
4. Why would a client choose this over hiring a freelancer, doing outreach in-house, or using a creator marketplace?
5. Who is the ideal client? Give their size, stage, budget, industry, and the job title of the decision-maker.
6. What are the biggest problems, frustrations, and fears this client has about influencer marketing? Use the course's exact words.
7. What has this client usually already tried and been disappointed by?
8. What does this client want most? Give the outcome, not the service.
9. What objections will the client raise before buying (price, trust, "will it work", "we can do it ourselves")? Give the course's recommended answer to each.
10. What events or moments trigger a client to finally buy?
11. What concrete results and benefits does the client get? Include numbers, timelines, and examples.
12. What work, time, and effort does the client NOT have to do?
13. What is the realistic timeline from signing to the first creator introduction, and to live content?
14. What risks does the client avoid (overpaying, bad-fit creators, fake audiences, no-shows)? How does a good agency remove each one?
15. What proof points or social-proof angles does the course suggest? What can a brand-new agency with no track record use?
16. What offer and pricing structure does the course recommend (retainer, flat fee, commission, success fee)? Give typical price ranges.
17. What guarantees or risk-reversals does the course recommend?
18. What is the recommended low-friction first step for a new client (free call, audit, sample list, pilot)? Why?
19. How does the course say to explain pricing to a client?

## Prompt 2: Process, creators, market, messaging, and trust

I'm rewriting the website copy for an influencer-marketing brokerage business that connects SaaS and AI brands with creators, based on this course. Answer from the course only, using its own wording, examples, and numbers. If something isn't covered, write "NOT COVERED" and don't guess. Use bullet points and keep each answer under 200 words.

1. Walk through the full client journey from first contact to the end of a campaign. For each step, say what the client does and what we do.
2. How are creators found, vetted, and approached? What makes a creator a good fit?
3. What does a "warm introduction" mean in this course, and why is it better than cold outreach?
4. What does the client receive as deliverables (a list, a call, a contract, a report)?
5. Why would a creator want to work with us? What do creators care about most?
6. What do creators in the AI, SaaS, and automation niche typically charge, by platform and size? What counts as a "fair market rate"?
7. What are the most common ways brands and creators misunderstand each other, and how does the broker fix them?
8. Why is influencer marketing for AI, SaaS, and B2B tech valuable or growing right now? Give stats and trends.
9. Why does the course say technical or B2B creators work better for software brands than mainstream influencers?
10. Which platforms does the course recommend for B2B and SaaS (YouTube, LinkedIn, X, newsletters), and why?
11. What headlines, hooks, taglines, or positioning statements does the course suggest for this kind of agency?
12. How does the course say to speak to clients? Give the tone, words to use, and words to avoid.
13. What mistakes does the course say agencies make in how they present themselves?
14. Give me 10 example phrases or sentences from the course that describe the value of this service to a client.
15. What call-to-action does the course recommend? What do the best-converting ones look like?
16. How should a new agency build trust before it has clients?
17. What credentials, guarantees, or transparency signals matter most to buyers?
18. List every statistic or number in the course that could be quoted in marketing copy, with its context.
19. What are the 5 most important things a potential client must understand within 10 seconds of landing on the website?
20. Is there anything important about how this business should present itself that I haven't asked about?
