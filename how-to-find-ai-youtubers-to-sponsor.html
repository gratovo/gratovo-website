How to Find AI YouTubers to Sponsor (and Which Ones Are Worth It)

Search like your buyer, shortlist ten, cut to three, and send a message worth answering.

By the Gratovo team · Updated October 8, 2026 · 2 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start from your buyer's problem, not from a list of famous channels.
  • Search YouTube the way your buyer does, and note who keeps showing up.
  • Shortlist about ten channels, then cut to three after checking real numbers and fit.
  • Read earlier sponsored videos to see how a creator treats a product.
  • Keep the first message short and specific, or hand the whole search to a broker.

Start with the viewer, not the creator

Write down who uses your product, what job it does, and what they type into YouTube when they look for help. A product that automates support tickets is searched for differently from one that helps developers write tests.

Search the way your buyer searches

  • Search for the problem you solve, in the words a customer would use.
  • Search for 'best AI tools for' plus the job your product does.
  • Search for tutorials of tools similar to yours and tools next to yours.
  • Note which channels appear again and again, and which have recent uploads.

Open a channel's Videos tab and sort by popular, then by latest. If one video has ten times the views of the rest, that is a one-off hit, not what a sponsorship will typically reach.

Look at who already covers your category

Videos that carry a paid-promotion label or a sponsor segment show which creators accept sponsorships and how they present them. Watch a few. A good integration explains the product in the creator's own words and shows it working.

Shortlist ten, then cut to three

For each channel, check average views on recent uploads, the quality of the comments, and where the audience lives. The method is in how to vet an AI creator before you sponsor them. A short final list is easier to compare, and easier to repeat with.

Check fit before you write

  • Topic: does the creator already cover this kind of problem?
  • Level: does the audience skew beginner, practitioner or developer, and is that your buyer?
  • Quality: are the last few videos well made and consistent?
  • Conflicts: have they recently promoted a direct competitor?

Make the first message short and specific

Most creators list a business contact in the About tab of the channel. A message that gets answered usually has five parts: who you are, what the product does in one line, why you picked this creator, what you propose, and how to reply. Skip the long deck. Mention a video you actually watched.

What the search really costs

Searching, checking each channel, writing to ten creators and chasing replies takes days of attention. That is the work a broker takes over.
